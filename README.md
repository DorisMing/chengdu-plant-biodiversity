# Chengdu Plant Biodiversity: A GBIF-Based Study

## 1. Project Overview

This project investigates the spatial distribution and biodiversity patterns of plant species in Chengdu, Sichuan Province, China, using occurrence records from the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF).

The project aims to develop a reproducible ecological data analysis workflow, from biodiversity data acquisition and cleaning to spatial analysis and visualization.

## 2. Research Background

Understanding the spatial distribution of plant biodiversity is essential for assessing regional biodiversity patterns and informing conservation planning.

Chengdu provides an interesting study area for exploring plant biodiversity across an urbanizing landscape with diverse environmental and land-use conditions.

By integrating biodiversity occurrence records with spatial information, this project will explore how plant species are distributed across the study region.

## 3. Research Objectives

The initial objectives are to:

- Compile plant occurrence records from GBIF for Chengdu.
- Assess data quality and identify potential spatial and taxonomic errors.
- Explore the spatial distribution of recorded plant species.
- Examine patterns of observed species richness across the study area.
- Develop reproducible workflows for biodiversity data analysis and visualization.

## 4. Research Questions

This project will initially explore the following questions:

1. What is the spatial distribution of recorded plant species in Chengdu?
2. Which areas have relatively high or low observed plant species richness?
3. How do sampling patterns and data quality influence the interpretation of biodiversity patterns?

## 5. Data Sources

**Primary data source:** Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF).

- Data type: Plant occurrence records.
- Geographic focus: Chengdu, Sichuan Province, China.
- Data access: GBIF-mediated occurrence download.
- Dataset DOI:https://doi.org/10.15468/dl.3evnqm

All GBIF data will be appropriately cited in accordance with GBIF data-use guidelines.

## 6. Methodological Workflow

The planned workflow includes:

1. Data acquisition and documentation.
2. Taxonomic and geographic data quality assessment.
3. Data cleaning and preprocessing.
4. Spatial distribution analysis.
5. Species richness assessment.
6. Data visualization and interpretation.

Potential tools include Python, GIS software, and spatial analysis libraries.
